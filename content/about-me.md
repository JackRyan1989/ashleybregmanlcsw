 I’m a licensed clinical social worker with a master’s in social work from Bryn Mawr College. Before becoming a therapist, I spent several years working in academic and nonprofit spaces—places that nurtured my love of stories and the many ways we try to make sense of our lives. That same curiosity and reverence for the human experience now guide my work in the therapy room.

 As a neurodivergent parent of two young neurodivergent children, I come to this work not only as a clinician, but as someone who understands how layered and tender the work of healing can be. These parts of my life shape how I see the world and how I show up with clients—with curiosity, compassion, and a deep respect for the many ways people live and process their experiences.

 There’s no one way to heal—I draw from multiple modalities, including relational and psychodynamic therapy, somatic practices, and mindfulness, tailoring my approach to each person and each session. What stays constant is a spirit of collaboration, thoughtful attention, and a warm yet candid presence.
